# Peskas — History & Global Scaling Timeline

A chronological record of how the Peskas small-scale-fisheries platform was conceived, co-developed, and scaled from its 2013 Timor-Leste origins to its present multi-country deployment across Asia and Africa.

## Aim
Capture the full operational, institutional, and scaling history of Peskas (formerly PeskAAS – Peskas Automated Analytics System) in one place — based on project records, agreements, reports, and implementation experience — so the path from a national Timor-Leste monitoring system to a modular, multi-country, multi-ecology platform is traceable year by year. This is the time-ordered companion to the present-state Peskas overview, which delegates the chronology here.

## Scope
Covers 2013–present. Peskas is a digital fisheries information system developed by WorldFish, led by Alex Tilley. It originated in **Timor-Leste** as a national small-scale fisheries monitoring system and has since scaled across Asia and Africa, including deployment under the **Asia–Africa BlueTech Superhighway (AABS)** and **ProFishBlue (Malawi)**. The timeline below tracks programme funding and agreements, technical milestones (digital data collection, vessel tracking, automated R analytics pipelines), national policy uptake, the PeskAAS → Peskas transition (national system to modular platform), the research the monitoring data has enabled, and the country-by-country scaling and harmonisation effort. See <https://peskas.org/>.

## Timeline

### Overview
- **Peskas** (formerly PeskAAS – Peskas Automated Analytics System) is a digital fisheries information system developed by WorldFish, led by Alex Tilley.
- It originated in **Timor-Leste** 🇹🇱 as a national small-scale fisheries monitoring system and has since scaled across Asia and Africa, including deployment under: Asia–Africa BlueTech Superhighway (AABS); ProFishBlue (Malawi).
- This document records the full operational, institutional, and scaling history, based on project records, agreements, reports, and implementation experience.

### 2015 | Fisheries Sector Support Programme (FSSP) Phase I launched
- **3 June 2015** Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) signed between: Ministry of Agriculture and Fisheries (MAF), Timor-Leste; WorldFish (ICLARM).
- **July 2015** FSSP officially commenced; Norwegian MFA funding secured (≈ NOK 9.5 million).
- Core programme objectives:
  - Improve fisheries information for sustainable management
  - Develop sustainable small-scale fishing technologies
  - Participatory and pro-poor capacity development
  - Support fisheries strategy and policy development
- Early activities:
  - Institutional diagnostics
  - Capacity assessments
  - Preparatory work for fisheries data and monitoring reform

### 2016 | Early digital monitoring and stock assessment pilots
- Reef fish biomass surveys conducted:
  - Underwater Visual Census (UVC)
  - Training of MAF technical staff
- Early experimentation with:
  - Digital data handling
  - GPS-based fishing effort tracking
  - Improved catch monitoring workflows
- Evidence accumulated that legacy paper systems could not support modern fisheries governance or reporting.

### 2017 | Conceptualisation of PeskAAS
- Technical Working Group established under the Director General for Fisheries.
- Decision taken to design a digital, automated, near-real-time small-scale fisheries monitoring system.
- _PeskAAS_ (Peskas Automated Analytics System) conceptualised:
  - Tablet-based data collection (Kobo)
  - Cloud-based ingestion and analytics
  - Automated R pipelines
  - Integration of vessel tracking (Pelagic Data Systems)
- **December 2017**
  - Addendum to FSSP MOA signed: Programme extended to June 2018.
  - Matched funding from Schmidt Marine Foundation to provide and test 500 Pelagic Data Systems vessel tracking devices as part of a national scale pilot.
  - Legal and institutional fisheries reform explicitly added as an objective.

### 2018 | Operationalisation and national uptake (Timor-Leste)
- PeskAAS becomes fully operational:
  - Near-real-time catch and effort analytics
  - Automated dashboards for management
- Nationwide expansion of vessel tracking.
- Participatory consultations conducted across:
  - 27 landing sites
  - All coastal municipalities
- PeskAAS data directly informs:
  - Drafting of the National Fisheries Strategy (NFS)
  - Revisions to fisheries legislation
- 🏆 CGIAR Big Data Inspire Challenge award received: Recognition of PeskAAS as a scalable digital innovation.

### 2019 | Formal national adoption and transition to scaling
- Official national launch of **PeskAAS**: Adopted as Timor-Leste's national fisheries monitoring system.
- Government institutionalisation:
  - MAF hires dedicated fisheries enumerators
  - One enumerator per coastal municipality
  - Positions funded through FSSP II
- PeskAAS established as the primary data source for fisheries decision-making.
- External framing begins shifting from **PeskAAS** → **Peskas**: Reflecting modular, exportable system design.

### 2020 | PeskAAS consolidation, policy relevance, and evidence production
- **Timor-Leste | PeskAAS as an operational national monitoring system**
  - PeskAAS continued operating as Timor-Leste's national small-scale fisheries monitoring system under FSSP Phase 2.
  - Digital catch and effort data collection sustained across national landing sites using PeskAAS enumerator workflows (tablet-based Kobo forms).
  - Automated PeskAAS analytics pipelines (R-based estimation and validation) continued producing near real-time national fisheries production estimates.
  - PeskAAS outputs used internally by MAF to support fisheries planning, management discussions, and reporting needs.
- **Policy and international visibility using PeskAAS as a case study**
  - PeskAAS documented as a flagship digital fisheries monitoring system in the FAO–WorldFish ICT4SSF publication.
  - PeskAAS positioned internationally as an example of:
    - Near real-time SSF monitoring
    - Integration of digital landings data and vessel tracking
    - Open and scalable analytics pipelines aligned with FAO SSF Guidelines
- **Gender and fisheries visibility informed by PeskAAS-aligned data**
  - Publication of Ambio paper on the contribution of women's fisheries in Timor-Leste.
  - Findings drew on landings, effort, and complementary datasets generated alongside the national monitoring system.
  - Results highlighted structural blind spots in conventional monitoring, reinforcing the need to expand PeskAAS-style systems to include women's and invertebrate fisheries.
- **Fisheries–nutrition research foundations informed by monitoring data**
  - PeskAAS production data used to contextualise fisheries supply constraints in emerging fisheries–nutrition research.
  - Monitoring data increasingly framed as a foundational input to food systems and nutrition analysis beyond fisheries management alone.

### 2021 | Integration of PeskAAS data into research, experimentation, and platform transition
- Use of PeskAAS data to inform experimental and development research:
  - PeskAAS system used to monitor fish supply in the design of a cluster-randomized controlled trial (RCT) in Timor-Leste — <https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0269221>
  - Monitoring data supported understanding of baseline fisheries production, spatial distribution, and seasonal variability relevant to intervention design.
  - PeskAAS-informed analysis helped assess feasibility and risks associated with nearshore Fish Aggregating Device (FAD) deployment — <https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2019.00487/full>
  - PeskAAS as enabling infrastructure for nutrition-sensitive fisheries research.
- Transition from national system (PeskAAS) toward scalable platform (Peskas):
  - Conceptual transition underway from PeskAAS as a Timor-Leste–specific system to Peskas as a modular, reusable platform.
  - Core characteristics consolidated:
    - Separation of country-specific data collection from core analytics
    - Configurable taxonomies (species, gear, habitats)
    - Reusable estimation and reporting pipelines
  - This transition set the foundation for:
    - Subsequent impact assessment of Peskas (2022)
    - Multi-country scaling in Asia and Africa
    - Later integration into AABS and ProFishBlue programs
- **August 2021** New MOA signed between MAF and WorldFish:
  - Government financing secured (~USD 120k/year) covering trackers and data support.

### 2022 | Institutionalisation and continuity (Timor-Leste)
- **2022** Contract continuation signed: Services extended to end 2023.
- Peskas impact assessment — <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsci.2024.103684>

### 2023 | Peskas App ideation and Peskas scoping in Africa
- Discovery workshops and FGDs with fishers and government stakeholders for development of a Peskas app for improving fisher safety at sea under the GEF IkanAdapt Project.
- User-journey mapping with fishers, communities, and government; identification of gaps in fisher-facing services.
- Funding secured from FCDO to scale Peskas to Africa under AABS.

### 2024 | Training and pipeline development in Kenya, Tanzania, Mozambique, Malawi
- **Asia Africa Bluetech Superhighway Project: Kenya 🇰🇪, Zanzibar 🇹🇿, Mozambique 🇲🇿**
- **ProFishBlue Project — Malawi 🇲🇼**
  - malawi.peskas.org
- **Digital Coasts (AABS-WP1)**
  - Peskas modules adopted by Kenya and Zanzibar governments and WCS Kenya.
  - Hiring in-country scientists.
  - Catch form alignment and enumerator training (landing site lists, gear classifications, ecosystem categories, species naming conventions).
  - Zanzibar Peskas changelog: <https://github.com/WorldFishCenter/peskas.zanzibar.data.pipeline/releases>
  - Kenya Peskas changelog: <https://github.com/WorldFishCenter/peskas.kenya.data.pipeline/releases>
  - Mozambique Peskas changelog: <https://github.com/WorldFishCenter/peskas.mozambique.data.pipeline/releases>
  - Regional agenda:
    - Progression of fisheries data harmonisation initiatives
    - Development of WIOMSA special session proposal
    - Alignment with Nairobi Convention regional mechanisms

### 2025 | Scaling national and regional harmonisation, and launch of TRACKS
- Peskas deployed in Malawi under ProFishBlue as the first Peskas instance for inland fisheries.
- Live national instance launched: **malawi.peskas.org**
- Significance:
  - Confirms Peskas as a multi-country, multi-ecology platform
  - Scaling beyond Timor-Leste and Asia
  - Deployment beyond AABS programme boundaries
- Digital behaviour change experiment started across 30 sites in Kenya — <https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2025.1689512/full>

### 2026 | Systemisation of scaling, standards alignment, and API development
- Strategic shift toward **systematising scale and sustainability**:
  - Repeatable implementation workflows
  - Automated generation of documentary deliverables
  - Development of APIs for data sharing
  - Capacity development of local partners to interpret, use, and adapt Peskas modules for their needs
- Increased emphasis on FAO alignment and automated small-scale fisheries reporting pipelines.
- Supporting national, regional, and global fisheries governance as a guiding method of data harmonisation.
